In 2020 I lost $17,000 in the stock market.

It wasn't bad luck. It was bad decisions: no process, no sizing rules, no way to tell a good call from a lucky one. I learned to invest properly after that and rebuilt. But the lesson that stuck wasn't about stocks. It was that I'd never been taught how to decide.

## The Tesla money

This year I had $20,000 set aside to buy a Tesla in cash.

I'm not buying it. For 2027, that money is tuition for a different kind of education: one year of using poker as a decision lab.

## Why poker

Most of life's important decisions give slow, noisy feedback. You pick a career, a house, an investment, and find out years later whether it was right, with no clean way to separate your judgment from your luck.

Poker compresses that loop. Every hand is a decision under uncertainty with incomplete information, real money on the line, and an outcome you see in minutes. It forces the skill most people never practice: judging a decision by the quality of the thinking, not by how it turned out.

You can make the right call and lose. You can make the wrong call and win. Learning to tell the difference is the whole game, at the table and everywhere else.

## The setup

- **Cap:** $20,000 for the year, $1,666.67 a month. When a month's money is gone, I stop until the 1st. No borrowing forward.
- **Winnings:** swept out the same day and never played again.
- **Rules:** ten hard rules, including leaving at 3 buy-ins down, no alcohol at the table, and no sessions on short sleep. [The full list is on the hub page.](/nonzero)
- **Ledger:** exact dollars, published monthly. Losses posted the same way as wins.

I'm a bartender, not a poker pro. I'm starting with almost no experience. October through December is study and preparation only. Day 1 is January 1, 2027.

## What counts as winning

Not the money. The money gets tracked honestly, but it isn't the scorecard.

The scorecard is whether I come out of 2027 a measurably better decision-maker: clearer about expected value, calmer under pressure, and more honest about the difference between a good decision and a good outcome. Every week I'll take one poker lesson and apply it to a real decision outside the game. That transfer is the point.

The downside is capped at $20,000. The upside is open.

## Follow along

The live ledger, rules, and links to every platform are at [rudygalan.com/nonzero](/nonzero). One email goes out after each month closes with the numbers, my best and worst decision, and one lesson that carried over to life.
