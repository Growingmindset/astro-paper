## The short version

I'm Rudy Galan. I'm a bartender in San Antonio, I study markets and economics on my own terms, and I publish what I find. Right now that means three things:

- **[Base Rate](/newsletter)**, a free weekly letter on markets, risk, and decisions. I'm studying for the Series 65.
- **[Mr. Nonzero](/nonzero)**, a year of poker in 2027 with a hard $20,000 cap and a public ledger.
- **[Work](/work)**: building toward project management and owner's rep roles in energy and large-scale infrastructure. I'm pursuing the CAPM and Oracle Primavera P6.

The thread through all three is the same question: how do you make good decisions when you can't know how they'll turn out?

## How I got here

**Fourteen years behind the bar.** I've worked high-volume hotel bars since 2012, across seven-plus properties. The mechanics of the job are simple. The environment is the education: every night is a long, live read on how people communicate, negotiate, and decide. That shows up in markets exactly the same way.

![Rudy Galan behind the bar](/action-shot.jpg)

**The body before the balance sheet.** Before the pivot, I spent years training athletes, with certifications in exercise science and sports nutrition. I don't train clients anymore, but the habits stuck: measure everything, progress in small steps, and respect what compounds.

**School, then on purpose, no school.** I took 150+ college credits across biology, physics, and economics, then stopped. I'm not pursuing a degree. I'd rather learn by doing real projects in public, where the results are checkable.

**Money, the hard way.** In 2020 I lost $17,000 in the stock market. That loss is why I learned to invest properly. I now manage my own portfolio and share every position and thesis change in [Base Rate](/newsletter) as it happens, with no hindsight edits. The rules are in [How I Invest](/posts/how-i-invest).

## What I believe

- **Start from the base rate.** Before deciding this time is different, know how often it usually goes.
- **Order matters.** Cash flow, then compounding, then leverage. [Most people do it backwards.](/posts/wrong-order)
- **Grade the decision, not the result.** Good calls lose sometimes. Bad calls win sometimes. Only one of those is repeatable.
